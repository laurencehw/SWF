# Tweetstorm: Should the United States Have a Sovereign Wealth Fund?

*A thread laying out the case for and against — including the 2026 "AI dividend" debate. Companion to [A Strategic Investment Fund for America](US_SWF_Proposal_WhitePaper.md).*

---

## Thread A — The Pitch (Pro 🧵)

**1/**
Should the United States have a sovereign wealth fund? Trump ordered one in Feb 2025. Biden's team was quietly designing one before that. Now Bernie Sanders & Alex Bores want one funded by AI. Strange bedfellows. Here's the real case — pro AND con. 🧵

**2/**
The world changed. China has deployed ~$1T through Belt & Road. Saudi PIF runs ~$925B. Singapore's GIC + Temasek top $1T. They use patient state capital as a tool of statecraft. The US shows up with a patchwork of agencies and a $60B-capped DFC. We're bringing a knife to a capital fight.

**3/**
Private markets are the deepest in the world — but they can't price national strategic value. No PE fund will accept below-market returns to secure a critical-mineral supply chain or keep an allied port out of Beijing's hands. That gap is exactly what a well-designed SWF fills.

**4/**
It's not a slush fund or "free money." Done right it's an *institution*: invests for the long term, compounds returns for taxpayers, and gives Washington a way to co-invest in chips, critical minerals, energy, and infrastructure where markets fall short.

**5/**
And the AI argument has supercharged it. The pitch: modern AI was trained on humanity's collective knowledge — our books, code, art, journalism. If it generates trillions, why should the upside go only to a handful of billionaires? "When a public resource creates wealth, the public should share in it."

**6/**
Two flavors of the AI version:
• **Sanders:** public owns up to *half* of big AI firms via a one-time equity tax, with board seats & voting power.
• **Bores ("AI Dividend"):** a token/compute tax + *out-of-the-money warrants* + tax reform, paying a dividend that triggers IF AI displaces workers.

**7/**
The Bores warrant idea is genuinely clever: the public only cashes in if AI succeeds *enormously* — i.e., exactly the scenario where mass job loss makes a safety net most necessary. It's an insurance policy that pays out when you need it. No dilution unless they win big.

**8/**
Even the AI labs are nodding along. OpenAI floated a "public wealth fund… giving every citizen a stake in AI-driven growth." Anthropic has floated "national sovereign wealth funds with stakes in AI." When the senator *and* the CEOs agree on the shape of the answer, pay attention.

**9/**
Precedent works. Norway turned oil into a >$2T fund for its people. Alaska has paid residents an oil dividend for 40+ years. The model — convert a one-time windfall into a permanent, broadly-shared asset — is proven. AI could be the new oil.

## Thread B — The Pushback (Con 🧵)

**10/**
Now the hard part. A US SWF is one of those ideas where *design is destiny*, and most versions fail the design test. Here's what worries me. 🧵

**11/**
**Funding.** The US runs trillion-dollar deficits. SWFs are normally funded by *surpluses* (oil, trade). Funding one with borrowed money means leveraging the Treasury to play the market — sensible only if returns reliably beat ~4.5–5% borrowing costs. That spread is thin and can flip.

**12/**
**Politicization** is the killer risk. A pot of state capital is a magnet for cronyism, bailouts of favored firms, and "invest in my district" pressure. Malaysia's 1MDB looted billions. Without an iron-clad independent board, a US fund becomes a political ATM.

**13/**
**Market distortion.** A government buyer with strategic motives can crowd out private capital, prop up zombie firms, and pick winners badly. Government has a poor track record as a venture capitalist.

**14/**
On the AI version specifically — **the Sanders model alarms me.** Government owning 50% of frontier AI firms + board seats + power to "block decisions" isn't a wealth fund. It's nationalizing the commanding heights of the economy. That's industrial policy and state control, dressed as a dividend.

**15/**
**The regulator-owner conflict.** If the state owns big stakes in AI companies, can it impartially regulate them for safety, antitrust, and consumer harm? Owner and cop shouldn't be the same person. The incentive to protect "its" champions is corrosive.

**16/**
**Constitutional & flight risk.** A one-time 50% equity tax "paid in stock" invites Fifth Amendment takings and apportionment fights — years in court. And frontier AI capital, talent, and incorporation are mobile. Grab too hard and the industry reincorporates in friendlier jurisdictions.

**17/**
**Concentration risk.** Loading a national fund with a few sky-high-valuation, highly-correlated AI names is the opposite of prudent diversification. If the AI trade unwinds, so does the people's fund — right when displaced workers need it most.

**18/**
**The dividend might not show up.** A warrant-funded dividend pays only if AI wins enormously. If AI *underdelivers* (plausible!), the safety net is underfunded — yet you've already restructured the tax code and spent the political capital.

---

## Thread C — The Synthesis 🧵

**19/**
So where does that leave us? My read: the strategic case for a US SWF is real, the AI distributive case is newly powerful — but the *control* versions are dangerous. The winning design is disciplined, not maximalist.

**20/**
A credible US SWF needs: ✅ congressional statute (not an EO) ✅ independent board majority insulated from politics ✅ minority, non-controlling stakes only ✅ Santiago Principles transparency ✅ phased funding that proves returns before scaling ✅ a sunset & real oversight.

**21/**
On AI, take the **Bores path, not the Sanders path**: a *ring-fenced, non-controlling* sleeve built from out-of-the-money warrants + a modest compute levy, feeding a *contingency-triggered* citizens' dividend. Capture the upside; don't seize the steering wheel.

**22/**
Timing matters. The leverage to negotiate public warrants is highest *now* — while AI firms still need compute, energy, procurement, and liability frameworks from government. That window narrows fast once the value is captured. Act early, structure smart.

**23/**
Bottom line: the question was never "can the US build a sovereign wealth fund?" It clearly can. The question is whether it can build one *wisely* — with the governance discipline to make it an asset for the public rather than a prize for the powerful. Design is destiny.

### Quick reference: Pro vs. Con at a glance

| Pro | Con |
|-----|-----|
| Matches China/Gulf/Singapore in economic statecraft | US has deficits, not surpluses — funded by borrowing |
| Fills gaps private capital structurally can't | Magnet for politicization & cronyism (cf. 1MDB) |
| Lets the public share in AI-generated wealth | Sanders-style control = nationalizing AI, not a fund |
| Warrants = upside insurance against job loss | Regulator-owner conflict of interest |
| Proven model: Norway, Alaska | Concentration risk; dividend may never materialize |
| Compounds long-term returns for taxpayers | Constitutional (takings) & capital-flight risks |
